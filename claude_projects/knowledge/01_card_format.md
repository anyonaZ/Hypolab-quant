# HYPOLAB hypothesis card format

_Generated by HYPOLAB on 2026-10-11 - upload to: Scout & Writer, Critic. Do not edit by hand; regenerate instead._

A card is an economic IDEA first and an
expression second. HYPOLAB loads cards written in exactly this YAML format (a list with one item per card).

## Fields
| Field | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| id | yes | Upper-case, unique. Cards from the idea team start with `IE_` (e.g. `IE_OPERATING_LEVERAGE`) |
| title | yes | One line, the idea in plain words |
| category | yes | `auto` (from the data used) or one of: analyst, fundamental, model, pv, risk, earnings, imbalance, other, macro, option, news, sentiment, shortinterest, institutions, insiders, socialmedia, broker |
| family | yes | snake_case group of closely related ideas; alphas from one family correlate |
| horizon | yes | `fast` (days), `medium` (weeks to a quarter), `slow` (quarters to a year) |
| strength | yes | 3 = replicated in many markets, 2 = solid in papers, 1 = speculative |
| sign | yes | e.g. `"+1"` or `"+1 on minus past-week return"`: higher signal -> higher future return? |
| story | yes | The mechanism: who makes the mistake, and why prices adjust slowly |
| why_persists | yes | Why it is not arbitraged away: who is on the other side, what limits them |
| references | yes | Papers or sources; at least one |
| roles | yes | The data the idea needs, as CONCEPT names (see 02_concepts.md), never field ids. `{}` if only standard price/volume fields are used |
| ladder | yes | 1-3 rungs, simplest first. Each: `name`, `expr`, `teaches` |
| settings | yes | `{neutralization: SUBINDUSTRY, decay: 0, truncation: 0.08}` style |
| expected_turnover | yes | `[low, high]` as fractions, e.g. `[0.02, 0.15]` |
| watch_out | no | Known traps, a list of short lines |

## Rules for ladder expressions
- Use only operators from 03_operators.md, with named-only arguments named (e.g. `winsorize(x, std=4)`).
- Refer to data only as `{role}` placeholders (filled with real fields per region by HYPOLAB) or the standard
  fields that exist everywhere: close, open, high, low, vwap, volume, returns, adv20, cap, sharesout.
- `{dir:role}` becomes 1 or -1 from the field's description (use it when a vendor's direction is unclear).
- Quarterly data (fundamentals, estimates) needs `ts_backfill(x, 63)` or longer before time-series operators.
- Windows must mean something: 1 (one day), 2 (two days), 3 (three days), 5 (one week), 10 (two weeks), 21 (one month), 42 (two months), 63 (one quarter), 126 (half a year), 189 (three quarters), 252 (one year), 504 (two years), 756 (three years). A window like 37 or 113 is flagged.
- Compare with peers: `group_rank(..., subindustry)` or neutralization; bound the output (rank / group_rank / zscore).
- Rung 1 is the plainest test of the mechanism; later rungs each change ONE thing for an economic reason.

## Two real cards to copy the style from
```yaml
- id: FND_ACCRUALS
  title: Accruals (earnings not backed by cash)
  category: auto
  family: fnd_quality
  horizon: slow
  strength: 3
  sign: "-1 on accruals"
  story: >
    When reported earnings are much higher than operating cash flow, the difference
    (accruals) is made of accounting estimates that tend to reverse. Investors treat
    all earnings alike, so high-accrual firms disappoint later.
  why_persists: >
    Investors fixate on headline earnings; analysing accruals takes work.
  references:
    - "Sloan (1996) Do Stock Prices Fully Reflect Information in Accruals and Cash Flows about Future Earnings?, The Accounting Review"
  roles:
    ni: {concept: net_income}
    cfo: {concept: cfo}
    assets: {concept: assets}
  ladder:
    - name: core
      expr: "-group_rank((ts_backfill({ni}, 252) - ts_backfill({cfo}, 252)) / ts_backfill({assets}, 252), subindustry)"
      teaches: "Accruals scaled by total assets; short the high-accrual firms."
    - name: with-cash-quality
      expr: "-group_rank((ts_backfill({ni}, 252) - ts_backfill({cfo}, 252)) / ts_backfill({assets}, 252), subindustry) + group_rank(ts_backfill({cfo}, 252) / ts_backfill({assets}, 252), subindustry)"
      teaches: "Add cash return on assets: low accruals AND high cash generation."
  settings: {neutralization: SUBINDUSTRY, decay: 0, truncation: 0.08}
  expected_turnover: [0.01, 0.06]
  watch_out:
    - "The accrual anomaly weakened after 2000 in the USA; often stronger outside the US."
- id: ANL_REVISION_BREADTH
  title: Revision breadth (how many analysts raise vs cut)
  category: auto
  family: anl_revision
  horizon: medium
  strength: 3
  sign: "+1"
  story: >
    The share of analysts raising versus lowering estimates captures the direction of
    expert opinion without being distorted by one extreme forecast.
  why_persists: "As for revisions: gradual information flow."
  references:
    - "Hawkins, Chamberlin & Daniel (1984) Earnings Expectations and Security Prices, Financial Analysts Journal"
  roles:
    up: {concept: rev_up}
    down: {concept: rev_down, pair_with: up}
  ladder:
    - name: core
      expr: "group_rank((ts_backfill({up}, 21) - ts_backfill({down}, 21)) / (ts_backfill({up}, 21) + ts_backfill({down}, 21) + 1), subindustry)"
      teaches: "(ups - downs) / (ups + downs + 1). The +1 avoids dividing by zero."
    - name: smoothed
      expr: "ts_mean(group_rank((ts_backfill({up}, 21) - ts_backfill({down}, 21)) / (ts_backfill({up}, 21) + ts_backfill({down}, 21) + 1), subindustry), 21)"
      teaches: "A month of smoothing: breadth is noisy day to day."
  settings: {neutralization: SUBINDUSTRY, decay: 3, truncation: 0.08}
  expected_turnover: [0.05, 0.30]
  watch_out: ["Make sure 'up' and 'down' refer to the SAME item and horizon (e.g. FY1 EPS, 30 days)."]
```
